How it began

Built for a handful of people. 14,245 showed up.

Last Ramadan we made Ramadan Strivers for our close friends and family: one simple place to guide each other through the month, tick off our deeds, keep up with the Quran and hold each other to it. Then people shared it. Individuals across the globe used it, and so did a school and a mosque.

Every day of the month

Everything a good day of Ramadan is made of

Daily checklist

Tick your deeds. See your day take shape.

From Fajr to the night prayer: salah, sunnah prayers, adhkar, Quran, charity and character. Every deed carries points, and every one shows where it comes from.

  • Sources on every deed. The hadith or verse, its reference and its grading, one tap away.
  • Streaks and badges that reward showing up, not perfection.
  • Honest about real life. Not fasting today, travelling or unwell: the day adapts, and missed fasts are kept for later.
Quran and preparation

Finish the Quran, and arrive ready.

Log the pages you read and the app works out the pace you need to complete it in the month. Before Ramadan, a 30-day programme steadies your salah, your Quran and your habits, with a countdown to the first fast.

  • Pages per day and what is left, always in view.
  • Prayer times for any city, with 24 calculation methods, Qibla and a printable timetable.
  • Dates are estimates. The app says so, and reminds you to follow your local mosque for moon sighting or calendar.
New to Islam

A gentle first Ramadan.

A guide written for people at the beginning: what Ramadan is, who fasts, what breaks a fast, and how to make wudu and pray, one step at a time with the words to say.

  • 12 short chapters in plain words, with Quran and hadith references.
  • A shorter deed list that grows week by week instead of asking for everything on day one.
  • 40 words explained, from suhoor to Laylatul Qadr.
Children and families

The whole house, on one phone or many.

Kids mode swaps the long list for 13 deeds a child can own, lights a star in the night sky for each good day, and tells a short story every evening. Parents stay in charge.

  • Family profiles on a shared device, and a family goal to reach together.
  • Grown-up check with a family PIN before a child's day counts on a leaderboard.
  • Quizzes, flashcards, a printable chart and a certificate for Eid.
Compete in good

Race each other to what matters.

Join with a six-letter code and your points appear on your group's leaderboard. Classes and teams compete on averages, so a small group can beat a large one.

  • A shared goal, such as 3,000 Quran pages together, and a charity target beside it.
  • Weekly awards: most improved, most consistent, most Quran read.
  • Only a display name is shared. Never which deeds you ticked.
Schools, mosques and communities

Run a Ramadan challenge without running a spreadsheet.

Create a competition in a minute, share the code or a QR poster, and watch your community take part. Everything an organiser needs is in one tab.

Helpers with the right access

Add colleagues as admin, teacher of one class, or viewer of reports only. Every change is logged.

Weekly awards and reports

The week's top three, most improved and top class, with a one-page report to print for assembly or the notice board.

Your goal, your deeds

Set a shared goal, double-points days and up to six deeds of your own, like helping to set up iftar.

QR check-in and sign-up lists

A daily door poster for Tarawih or class attendance, and lists for iftar dishes and volunteers.

Made for children's privacy

Import a class list, print parent consent slips with each child's own join code, and switch on under-13 protections.

Leagues and head to head

Challenge another school for a week, or join a league table ranked by average points per active person.
