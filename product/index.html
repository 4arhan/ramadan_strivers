Everything, explained

What a good day of Ramadan is made of

Eight parts of the app, what each one does, and the numbers behind it. Every screen below was taken from the app itself.

1 · Daily checklist

Tick your deeds. See your day take shape.

From the two rakahs before Fajr to the last istighfar before sleep: salah, sunnah prayers, adhkar, Quran, charity, character and the things to stay away from. Tick what you did and the day adds itself up.

  • 68deeds to tick
  • 7groups
  • 67cite a hadith or verse
  • 12badges
  • The source, one tap away. Switch on detail mode and the deeds show the hadith or verse behind them, with the reference, the grading and a short explanation.
  • Points that respect the last ten nights. Each deed is worth 5 to 15 points, and from day 21 points count one and a half times, with an extra group of deeds for seeking Laylatul Qadr.
  • Streaks and badges for showing up. 4 streaks follow Fajr, Isha, Quran and charity, because the most beloved deeds are the consistent ones.
  • Honest about real life. Mark a day as fasted, a child's practice fast, or not fasting with the reason, and missed fasts are kept in a make-up planner. Sisters' mode sets prayer and fasting aside during your period.
  • One line of reflection a day, saved with the day and printed in your Eid report.
2 · Prayer times and dates

The right time, wherever you are.

Search for any city or town in the world, use your location or type in coordinates. The app suggests the method most mosques in your country follow, then counts down to iftar and to the end of suhoor.

  • 23calculation methods
  • 563cities ready offline
  • 5daily prayers, plus sunrise
  • Match your mosque exactly. Hanafi or standard Asr, high-latitude rules for long summer twilight, your own angles, and a few minutes of adjustment for each prayer.
  • Qibla direction from where you stand, and a month timetable you can print for the fridge.
  • Dates are shown as estimates. The app follows the Umm al-Qura calendar, lets you shift it a day for your local moon sighting, and reminds you to check with your mosque.
  • Put Ramadan in your calendar, with the 30 days, iftar times and Eid, in one download.
3 · Quran tracker

Finish the Quran, a few pages at a time.

Log the pages you read after each sitting. The tracker knows the Madani mushaf, shows which juz you are in, and works out the pace you need for the days that are left.

  • 604pages
  • 30juz
  • 20pages a day finishes it
  • Always know where you stand: pages today, pages needed each day, and days left.
  • A chart of your reading against the pace to finish, and a log of every day.
  • More than recitation. Five Quran deeds: reciting, reflecting, applying a lesson, listening and memorising.
  • Share the good. Turn your progress or the hadith of the day into an image for your family group or your story.
4 · Ramadan Prep

Arrive ready, not rushed.

A 30-day programme for the month before. It begins with a short check of where you are, then gives you three small actions a day: first your salah, then the Quran, then fasting and habits, and finally the heart, family and charity.

  • 30days
  • 90small actions
  • 6areas of your life
  • 7readiness questions
  • Daily check-ins for the five prayers and your minutes with the Quran, with a weekly milestone.
  • My Ramadan plan: your intentions, your dua list, and a planner for any fasts you still owe.
  • It keeps time with Sha'ban, so the last day of the plan is the day before Ramadan. A countdown on Today tells you how long is left.
5 · Learn

Know why, not only what.

Learn turns the hadith and verses behind the deeds into quick questions and flashcards. Five minutes after Fajr is enough.

  • 149true or false
  • 62multiple choice
  • 100flashcards in 7 decks
  • 10topics
  • Every answer shows its source, so a quiz is also a short lesson.
  • Flashcards that remember for you. Cards you know come back less often, cards you miss come back sooner.
  • Today's 5, a short daily mix that builds a learning streak.
  • Decks for every stage: Ramadan words, duas, verses on fasting, Sunnahs of fasting, Ramadan in history, Quran facts and preparing for the month.
6 · New to Islam

A gentle first Ramadan.

Written for people at the beginning. What Ramadan is, who fasts and who is excused, what breaks a fast, how to make wudu, and how to pray, one position at a time with the words to say.

  • 12short chapters
  • 9steps of wudu
  • 12steps of prayer
  • 40words explained
  • A list that grows with you. The starter checklist begins small and adds a little each week, instead of asking for everything on day one.
  • Unfamiliar word? Tap it. Suhoor, witr, tarawih and the rest are explained where you meet them.
  • Clear about its limits. It is general guidance with references, not a ruling, and it says when scholars differ and when to ask an imam.
7 · Children and families

The whole house, on one phone or many.

Kids mode, made for ages 8 to 12, swaps the long list for deeds a child can own, lights a star for each good day and tells a short story every evening. Parents stay in charge.

  • 13deeds for children
  • 30stories, one a day
  • 48easier questions
  • Grown-up check. With a family PIN, a child's day counts on a leaderboard only after a parent has looked at it.
  • Family profiles and a family goal. Several people share one device, each with their own checklist, Quran log and badges.
  • Short surahs to learn, a sticker chart for the fridge and a certificate for Eid.
  • Buddies. Send a private link to a grandparent or friend. They see a short summary, never your checklist or journal, and can send one of four kind messages.

Race each other to what matters.

Join with a six-letter code, a link or a QR code, choose a display name, and your points appear on your group's leaderboard. Classes and teams are ranked on averages, so a small group can beat a large one.

  • 6letters to join
  • 10prizes an organiser can set

Questions

Good to know before you start.

Is it really free?

Yes. There are no ads, no subscriptions and nothing to buy, for individuals and for organisations of any size.

Do I need to create an account?

No. Everything works without one and your data stays on your device. An account is optional: create one if you want a backup and to sync between your phone, tablet and computer. You can delete it and its cloud data at any time. Only people who organise a competition need an account, so that nobody else can manage it.

Does it work without the internet?

Yes. Once you have opened it, the app works offline, including the checklist, the Quran tracker, the lessons and prayer times for major cities. Leaderboards, sync between devices and the worldwide city search need a connection.

How does it know when Ramadan starts?

It follows the Umm al-Qura Hijri calendar and shows the start and end of Ramadan as estimates. The real dates depend on moon sighting or the calendar your community follows, so the app reminds you to check with your local mosque, and you can shift the date by a day in settings.

Where does the content come from?

Deeds, lessons and quiz answers cite verses of the Quran and hadith, with references you can follow to read the source yourself, and the deeds show how each hadith is graded. Translations of meanings are there to help you understand.

The app gives general guidance, not rulings. Schools of thought differ on some details, and it says so. For your own situation, ask a qualified imam or your local mosque.

Can children use it safely?

Yes. Kids mode is made for ages 8 to 12. A child never needs an email, an account or a phone number, usage statistics are always off in kids mode, and a parent can confirm each day with a family PIN. In a school competition the organiser can switch on protections for participants under 13 and print consent slips for parents.

What does a competition see about me?

Your display name, your class or team if you give one, and your totals: points, days logged, average completion and Quran pages. The organiser can also see your total for each day to keep things fair. Nobody sees which deeds you ticked, your journal or anything else.

How does a school or mosque get started?

Open the app, go to Compete and choose Organise. Create a free account, name your organisation, add prizes if you have them, and share the six-letter code or print the QR poster. Participants join with the code and a display name. It takes about a minute, and you can add helpers, goals and sign-up lists afterwards.

I am not fasting or praying at the moment. Is it still for me?

Yes. Mark a day as not fasting with the reason, whether illness, travel or your period, and the fast is kept in a make-up planner. Sisters' mode sets prayer and fasting deeds aside so your percentage reflects the worship that applies to you: dhikr, dua, listening to the Quran, charity and character.

Can several people share one phone?

Yes. Family profiles let each person keep their own checklist, Quran log and badges on the same device, and a child's profile opens straight into kids mode.

Is it only useful in Ramadan?

No. In Sha'ban it becomes Ramadan Prep, with a countdown to the first fast. For the rest of the year it tracks the Sunnah fasts on Mondays and Thursdays, the white days, the six days of Shawwal and the first ten days of Dhul Hijjah, and the Quran tracker and lessons work all year.

Which languages is it in?

English, Arabic, Urdu, Bengali, Indonesian, Malay, Turkish, Persian, Hindi, French, Spanish, Portuguese, Russian, Swahili and Chinese. Arabic, Urdu and Persian are laid out right to left. You can change the language at any time in settings.
